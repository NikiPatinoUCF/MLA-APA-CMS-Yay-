📘 MLA In-Text Citations

Basic Format: Author-Page

MLA uses a parenthetical citation with the author's last name and page number. No comma between them.

✓ Example: According to Smith, "the results were significant" (23).

Author Named in Signal Phrase

If you mention the author's name in your sentence, only include the page number in parentheses.

✓ Example: Smith argues that "the evidence is clear" (45).

Two Authors

For two authors, include both last names separated by "and".

✓ Example: Recent studies confirm this theory (Smith and Jones 34).

Three or More Authors

Use the first author's last name followed by "et al." (no italics).

✓ Example: The research demonstrates clear patterns (Johnson et al. 67).

No Page Numbers (Websites)

For sources without page numbers (like websites), use only the author's name.

✓ Example: The organization states that "climate action is urgent" (Smith).
💡 Pro Tip: The period comes after the parenthetical citation, not before it!

📗 APA In-Text Citations

Basic Format: Author-Year

APA uses author-date format with a comma. Page numbers are required for direct quotes.

✓ Example: The study found significant results (Smith, 2020, p. 45).

Author Named in Sentence

When the author is named in the text, put the year in parentheses immediately after the name.

✓ Example: Smith (2020) argued that "the evidence is clear" (p. 45).

Two Authors

Use "and" in narrative citations and "&" in parenthetical citations.

✓ Narrative: Smith and Jones (2020) found significant results.

✓ Parenthetical: The results were significant (Smith & Jones, 2020).

Three or More Authors

Use the first author's name followed by "et al." from the first citation onwards.

✓ Example: Research confirms this pattern (Johnson et al., 2021).

Page Numbers

Use "p." for one page and "pp." for multiple pages. Page numbers are required for direct quotes.

✓ One page: "Direct quote here" (Smith, 2020, p. 23).

✓ Multiple pages: "Longer quote" (Jones, 2019, pp. 45-47).
💡 Pro Tip: Always include the year, even when you mention it in your text!

📙 Chicago (CMS) In-Text Citations

Two Systems: Notes or Author-Date

Chicago offers two citation systems. Notes-Bibliography uses footnotes or endnotes. Author-Date uses parenthetical citations similar to APA.

Notes-Bibliography System (Footnotes)

Use superscript numbers in text that correspond to footnotes at the bottom of the page.

✓ In text: According to recent research, climate change is accelerating.¹

✓ Footnote: 1. John Smith, The Great Novel (New York: Penguin Press, 2020), 45.

Shortened Footnotes (Subsequent References)

After the first full citation, use a shortened form with author's last name and page number.

✓ First reference: 1. John Smith, The Great Novel (New York: Penguin Press, 2020), 45.

✓ Later references: 2. Smith, The Great Novel, 67.

Author-Date System

Similar to APA, with author's last name, year, and page number in parentheses.

✓ Example: The evidence is clear (Smith 2020, 45).
💡 Pro Tip: Choose one system (Notes or Author-Date) and use it consistently throughout your paper!

🎯 Practice: Identify the Correct Citation

Question 1 (MLA): Which is the correct in-text citation for a direct quote from page 56 by author John Smith?

a) "Quote here." (Smith 56)
b) "Quote here" (Smith 56).
c) "Quote here" (Smith, 56).
d) "Quote here." (John Smith 56)

Question 2 (APA): Which is correct when the author is named in your sentence?

a) Smith argued that "results were clear."
b) Smith (2020) argued that "results were clear" (p. 23).
c) Smith argued that "results were clear" (2020, p. 23).
d) Smith, 2020, argued that "results were clear" (23).

Question 3 (MLA): How do you cite three or more authors in MLA?

a) (Smith, Jones, and Johnson 45)
b) (Smith et al. 45)
c) (Smith and others 45)
d) (Smith, et al., 45)

📊 Quick Comparison

📘 MLA

(Author Page)

Simple and clean. No punctuation between author and page number.

📗 APA

(Author, Year, p. Page)

Emphasizes publication date. Uses commas and "p." for pages.

📙 CMS

Footnotes or (Author Year, Page)

Two systems available. Notes-Bibliography is common in humanities.
